# Glow Halal — Catalogue Expansion Keyword Sheet (Pakistan, Aug 2026)

> Produced from 32 live Google-autocomplete probes (gl=pk), 8 SERP checks, competitor page audits
> and DRAP regulatory grounding. House rules applied throughout: no cure claims, no "halal certified",
> no fake reviews, EN/Roman-Urdu keyword deconfliction (no phrase used on both sides).

## THE ONE BIG FINDING

**Roman Urdu SERPs in Pakistan are structurally empty.** Roman-Urdu queries ("balon ke liye behtareen
tel", "beri ke patte ka sabun") are served Indian Hindi content in Devanagari script that most
Pakistanis cannot read. No major PK herbal competitor (Saeed Ghani, ChiltanPure, Hemani, Marhaba,
Qarshi) publishes Roman Urdu content. Our `/ur-roman/blog/` structure is the only purpose-built asset
for this gap — it is where a zero-authority domain wins first.

## 1. Competitor landscape (openings)

- **Saeed Ghani** — owns branded soap queries; English only, zero Roman Urdu, brand-first pages.
- **ChiltanPure** — 2,500-word pages that rank, but make flagrant medical claims ("Increase
  Testosterone") — YMYL-vulnerable; our honest pages are the long-term bet against them.
- **Conatural** — urban-elite English positioning; ignores the Roman-Urdu mass market.
- **Hamdard/Qarshi** — licensed legacy supplement brands; institutional sites, weak content marketing.
- **Salajeet sellers** — crowded, but almost none address the #1 buyer anxiety honestly: fakes.
  Authenticity content ("asli salajeet ki pehchan") is our wedge.
- **Nobody in PK sells or ranks a beri/sidr leaf soap** — "beri ke patte ka sabun" autocomplete exists
  with an empty PK SERP. Sunnah-association product, zero competition → launch SKU candidate.

## 4. Compliance rules of engagement

- **DRAP floor:** herbal/unani supplements must be enlisted; consumer advertising of enlisted products
  needs prior DRAP approval; cure/treat/prevent claims prohibited. Owner rule: **sell only
  DRAP-enlisted supplements** and cite the enlistment number on the page as a trust signal.
- Soaps = cosmetic language only ("for acne-prone skin", never "cures acne"). No whitening promises.
- Creams = "helps the appearance of", never "dagh khatam". Burns mentions always lead with
  cool-water-10-20-min + doctor.
- Oils = "hair fall control" cosmetic framing ok; "ganjapan ka ilaj" only as an honest myth-buster.
- Supplements = herb education + authenticity + side-effects honesty; never potency/disease claims.
