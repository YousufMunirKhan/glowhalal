## How to Identify Original Lookman-e-Hayat Oil: 5 Checks Before You Buy

Search for Lookman-e-Hayat oil on any marketplace in Pakistan and you will see the same word repeated in listing after listing: "Original". "New Original". "100% Original Imported". When every seller has to say it, it usually means buyers have learned to ask — because copies and refilled bottles exist wherever a trusted product sells for decades.

This guide gives you five practical checks. None of them requires opening the bottle.

### 1. Check the manufacturer's name on the label

Genuine Lookman-e-Hayat Tel is made by **M.U. Amrelia**, a manufacturer based in Mumbai, India, and the name appears on the packaging. If the label shows no manufacturer at all, a different company name, or only an importer's sticker covering the printing underneath, treat that as a red flag.

The manufacturer also maintains an official website with product photography. If you are unsure about a bottle you already have, compare your label against the manufacturer's own images — spelling, layout and colours should match exactly. Copies most often get small things wrong: letter spacing, the shade of the label, or the spelling of "Lookman" itself.

### 2. Look for a printed batch number and expiry date

A legitimately manufactured herbal oil carries a **batch number and expiry date printed on the pack** — printed by machine, not on a paper sticker someone could apply later. No batch number, no expiry, or a sticker where printing should be means you cannot verify what is inside or how old it is. Walk away.

This is also your practical check after delivery: the expiry date should give you comfortable time to use the bottle.

### 3. The seal must be intact — and you should refuse a broken one

Buy only a **sealed** bottle. If you ordered online with Cash on Delivery, you can inspect the seal when the parcel arrives — that is one of the quiet advantages of COD. A loose cap, oil residue around the neck, or a seal that looks re-wrapped are all reasons to refuse the parcel and pay nothing.

### 4. Judge the seller, not just the listing

A listing can say anything. Signals worth more than the word "original":

- **Does the seller publish the full composition?** Genuine Lookman-e-Hayat Tel has a declared formula of **97% til (sesame) seed oil and 3% guggul (Commiphora mukul)**. A seller who states this plainly — and states what is *not* in it — is describing a product they actually know.
- **Does the seller make honest claims?** This is a traditional herbal massage oil. A seller promising medical cures is either careless or counting on you not to check. Honesty elsewhere in a shop is evidence about the bottle too.
- **Can you reach them?** A real phone number that answers on WhatsApp before you order will also answer after.

### 5. Be suspicious of prices that are too good

As of 2026, a genuine 100ml bottle in Pakistan typically sells in the **Rs 2,000–2,300 range** on major marketplaces. You will also find listings claiming two bottles for Rs 1,400 — roughly a third of the going rate. An imported product cannot honestly be sold at a third of the market price. A deep "discount" on an imported herbal oil is not a bargain; it is the single most common signal of a refill or copy.
